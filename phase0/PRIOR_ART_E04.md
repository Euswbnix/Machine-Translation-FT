# Prior-art assessment: repetitiveness in the QE top band (WMT14 en-fr, CometKiwi-22)

Basis: the 37 checker-verified papers only. "Pre-empted" verdicts are firm. "Open" verdicts are provisional: they rest on absence within this list, and several system papers named in the checkers' notes were never opened (section 5).

## 1. Verdict

**The bare phenomenon is pre-empted.** That a narrow QE top slice is no better, or worse, than a same-size random draw or a lower band is already in print:
- Waldendorf et al. 2025: CometKiwi-22 top 1% vs same-size random, 3 seeds.
- Chimoto et al. 2024: COMET-QE pruning below random on en-de.
- Tan et al. 2024: higher-QE band worse than lower band at matched size, LLM fine-tuning.
- Finkelstein et al. 2024: top-0.3% BLEURT-QE slice collapses, no random control.

It must be credited, not claimed.

**C1 (QE top-k preferentially selects repetitive, lexically thin text): partially pre-empted in qualitative form; open as a measured claim.**
- Lo et al. 2018 (NRC, WMT18) reports "much redundancy and a somewhat limited vocabulary coverage in the top-ranking sentences" of its sentence-pair scorers.
- Bernier-Colborne & Lo 2019 says the same of YiSi-2, a scorer that paper itself calls a QE metric.
- Wang et al. 2018 and Xu et al. 2020 state it as a possibility; Waldendorf et al. 2025 (App. C) notes it anecdotally for their own filter.
- None measures it, and none uses a learned neural QE metric on a curated pool. "First to notice" is unavailable; "first to measure for CometKiwi" is, on this list.

**C2 (repetitiveness, not domain mismatch, causes the out-of-domain harm): open as a controlled result; its general form is partially pre-empted.**
- No verified paper tests repetitiveness against domain mismatch, runs a length-matched split of a selected band, or contrasts in-domain with out-of-domain.
- Saunders & DeNeefe 2024 (EMNLP main) already argues that loss of generic quality after NMT fine-tuning tracks a lexical property of the fine-tuning set (generic-vocabulary coverage), not distribution divergence or pair count. That evidence is correlational, across domains, and involves no QE selection.
- Lo et al. 2018 offers a one-clause untested conjecture (not enough varied data).
- Chimoto 2024 and Waldendorf 2025 offer different, untested explanations. These are competing accounts to address, not pre-emption.

**C3, first half (QE selection needs a diversity/redundancy control): pre-empted as an idea.**
- Principle: Kirchhoff & Bilmes 2014 (per-sentence top-N cannot represent redundancy).
- Implemented on top of quality rankings for MT data: Lo et al. 2018, Azpeitia et al. 2018, Lu et al. 2018, Bernier-Colborne & Lo 2019, Xu et al. 2020; recorded as practice in Koehn et al. 2019.
- Restated outside NMT filtering: Pan et al. 2024, Bukharin et al. 2024, Wettig et al. 2024, Zheng et al. 2023.
- The prior evidence that such controls help NMT is weak to negative (section 4).

**C3, second half (a pre-training repetitiveness statistic predicts the harm): partially pre-empted in general form; open for an n-gram redundancy statistic on QE-selected data.**
- Saunders & DeNeefe 2024 is the precedent for "a statistic computable before training predicts fine-tuning loss", using coverage rather than redundancy.
- No verified paper evaluates a redundancy statistic as a predictor of harm.

## 2. What is actually new

Narrowest statement that survives:

> For a learned reference-free QE metric (CometKiwi-22) on a curated high-resource pool, a pre-registered, seeded, controlled test: inside the QE top band, at matched size and identical source-length histograms, the half with higher cross-row source 8-gram overlap costs 2.73 BLEU more on out-of-domain news (seed sd 0.28) and nothing on in-domain text, while the domain-mismatch explanation failed its own pre-registered test.

Two supporting pieces also appear new on this list:
- A measurement of the top band's lexical thinness against a matched random draw (type/token ratio, duplicate rate) for a learned QE metric. Prior statements are unquantified remarks.
- A matched-size, 3-seed top/random/bottom comparison when fine-tuning a converged NMT model. Prior matched-random evidence is from-scratch training or single-run LLM fine-tuning.

What the evidence given to this pass does not license:
- **"QE metrics preferentially select" as a general property.** There is one pool, one pair, one metric, and a band that is 77% UN.
- **"Repetitiveness is the cause of the top-vs-random gap."** The split shows repetitiveness modulates harm within the band. It does not show it accounts for the 0.79 BLEU gap to random.
- **"A redundancy control fixes QE selection."** No intervention result was given to this pass.
- **"Predicts."** One median split is a two-point contrast, not a predictive relationship.

## 4. Risks a reviewer will raise

1. **"The headline is known."** Four papers already show a QE top slice failing against random or a lower band. Without the mechanism result the paper has no contribution.
2. **"NRC said this in 2018 and 2019."** The qualitative C1 and the remedy both exist. The answer has to be measurement and control, and the paper must not use "first to observe" language.
3. **"It is the pool, not the metric."** The band is 77% UN. Chimoto 2024 shows the sign of QE-vs-random flips between datasets. Waldendorf 2025 shows it flips with language pair and kept fraction (Kiwi beats random at 10% and above on En-De; our band is about 6%). One pool cannot support "QE metrics preferentially select repetitive text"; a second pool or pair is needed, or a demonstration that the association holds inside a single domain.
4. **"Repetitiveness is domain narrowness by another name."** Fan et al. 2025 ties diversity loss to domain-specific selection, and QuRating's account is distributional coverage. An in-domain null with an out-of-domain gap can be read as "the repetitive half is more deeply UN". The paper must define domain mismatch operationally and report the UN share of each half.
5. **"The split explains variation inside the band, not the gap to random."** A 2.73 BLEU gap between halves does not show that repetitiveness accounts for the 0.79 gap between top band and random. On the numbers given, the diverse 242k half (-2.54) sits below the 927k random draw (-0.90), but at a different size. A random control at 242k is needed before claiming a redundancy control rescues QE selection.
6. **"The remedy is old and does not reliably work."** Vicomtech lost up to 5.49 average BLEU for NMT. RWTH gained 0.0 to +0.4 under ranking. Alibaba gained +0.33 on one run at 10M words and nothing at 100M. Volctrans gained +0.03 to +0.19. NRC's gain vanished at 100M words. C3's prescription needs an intervention result of its own.
7. **"Diversity stops mattering at scale."** G-DIG, Alibaba and NRC all report the benefit fading as the selection grows. Our 927k band contradicts that, and the likely explanation is again the UN block, which returns to risk 3.
8. **"Duplicates are not the story."** Waldendorf's deficit persists after aggressive near-duplicate removal. Show the effect survives exact deduplication of the band (4.14% duplicate rows).
9. **"Your statistic is a proxy for vocabulary coverage."** Saunders & DeNeefe is the obvious comparison. Compute their coverage on both halves.
10. **"Length."** Zhang et al., Chimoto & Bassett 2022 and Wang et al. 2018 all make length the default suspect. The split answers it. The descriptive type/token comparison (0.007 vs 0.016) is not stated to be length-controlled and is length-sensitive.
11. **"This is catastrophic forgetting, not data selection."** Every condition, including random, lowers BLEU. The phenomenon papers train from scratch; the fine-tuning precedent is the forgetting literature. Claims about "QE-based data selection" in general need a from-scratch result or must be scoped to fine-tuning.
12. **"Predicts" is too strong.** One median split is not a predictive relationship. Saunders & DeNeefe at least report rank correlations across eight domains. A dose-response across finer bins would be the minimum.
13. **Competing explanations are unaddressed.** Chimoto 2024 points to data quality (length variance, foreign-language sentences); Waldendorf 2025 to metric training coverage. Neither is tested there, but both need a sentence.
